# Ruling record — increment 0.3, the architecture of record

> Written because the junior's comprehension pass found that `PROGRESS.md`
> cited verdicts no one could verify: the rulings existed only in an
> orchestration transcript, not in the tree. A record that cites an
> unverifiable judgement is the same defect as a check that cannot fail.
>
> **Convention from here:** every gate verdict that a `PROGRESS.md` entry cites
> gets a file in `docs/rulings/`. The verdict line is quoted verbatim. If a
> ruling is not written down, `PROGRESS.md` must say `Proof: NOT PROVIDED`.

## Round 1 — the first draft (805 lines)

**Ringmaster: `GO WITH CONDITIONS`** — nine conditions.

> *"the four-box shape and its realm split are the right structure for 'the
> whole harness is a static page,' but the realm claim is oversold, the Door is
> unbuildable on this protocol, the flow table arrives two waves before it is
> earned, and the line ratchet cannot run as written."*

The nine: (1) downgrade the two-media claim to three mechanisms of different
strength; (2) a banner rule for `src/adapters/**`; (3) add `config/probe` or
overrule the Door; (4) reconcile with `DESIGN.md` in writing; (5) move the flow
table to 4.5; (6) cut `attachments` and `timeoutMs`; (7) name when the ratchet
arms and strike the false sharding claim; (8) buy the stub test, restate the
verbatim-prompt rule; (9) add the constitution rewrite to 0.4.

**Critic: 10 BLOCKERs, 15 MAJORs, 8 MINORs.** The load-bearing ones:

- `FromEngine` omitted every reply `ToEngine` promised, and the protocol check
  derives its universe from `FromEngine` — the declared-but-never-emitted
  defect class, inside the check built to catch it.
- `appendMessage` took a record whose `seq` the *caller* computed, so
  append-only at the store was read-modify-write at the caller — the lost-update
  race the design claimed one line above was inexpressible.
- The purity rule banned the bare token `self`, but `"self-contained"` sits
  inside verbatim prompt bytes the constitution forbids editing. Confirmed
  against the oracle: `grep -c "self-" tests/golden/*.prompt` → 1, 1, 0.
- The realm check grepped three exact spellings; `typeof document === 'undefined'`
  or `'window' in globalThis` walks past all three. The open set is the
  violations, not the spellings.
- Three separate defects in the bundle check, two of which measurement later
  confirmed as fact.

## Round 2 — the revision (1436 lines)

**Ringmaster: `GO`** — all nine conditions met.

> *"all nine conditions met, the realm split now rests on measured physics
> rather than a diagnosis the compiler had already deleted, and wave 1 is
> unblocked once 0.4 rewrites the constitution."*

It ruled all six declared non-compliances legitimate, and ruled the TypeScript
re-argument honest rather than a rescue on three grounds: the error was conceded
at twice the size with the decomposition printed; the decomposition ran along
the axis that bears on the ruling; and a falsifiable counterfactual was stated
in the losing direction.

**Critic: 8 of 10 blockers and 14 of 16 majors fully resolved.** Two blockers
partial and four new defects introduced by the revision itself:

1. `CORE_MARK` proved `PromptAssembler` reached a chunk, not that *core* did —
   a leak importing `Slot` shakes the assembler away and the check goes green
   with core in the page chunk.
2. "Exactly the singleton set" fails on a legitimate `splitChunks` build.
3. **The lock lifetime claim was wrong** — see below.
4. The `total` ratchet defect, correctly diagnosed, was reintroduced one
   paragraph later for `max`.
5. The protocol union extraction mechanism was unstated, which would have made
   the totality check tautological.

## Round 3 — the fixes

All ten items landed. Three outcomes worth preserving:

**The lock lifetime, and a measurement that argued for a broken design.**
`navigator.locks.request` releases when the callback's promise *settles*, not
when the tab closes. The obvious implementation releases the instant
`openDatabase()` resolves and both tabs write. Worse: M5 was cited as evidence
the election works. It is not — the probe's callback returned, the lock
released, and that is exactly why the following `{ifAvailable:true}` was
granted. **The measurement quoted in the design's defence was consistent with
the design being broken.** M5 proves three API behaviours exist; it does not
prove the election, because the probe never implemented the election. PLAN 1.5
now asserts what M5 did not test: a second `{ifAvailable:true}` issued *while
the first callback is still pending* must receive `null`.

**The bundle check now claims only what it can prove.** `checks/layers.ts` is
primary and authoritative, proving `ui ↮ core` from the source import graph
where every import is visible whatever the bundler does. `checks/bundle.ts` is
a corroborator and its assertion changed from identity to reachability.

**The `max` ratchet mistake is on the record with its meta-lesson:** the fix for
`total` was reasoned about in isolation instead of being applied to every number
in the section.

## What the reviews cost, and what they bought

Three rounds, two reviewers, one orchestrator probe. No code was written against
any of the ten blockers. The two that would have shipped broken — the seq
allocator and the lock lifetime — are both concurrency defects that reproduce
only under a second tab or an overlapping turn, which is to say they are the two
most expensive kinds of bug to find later.
