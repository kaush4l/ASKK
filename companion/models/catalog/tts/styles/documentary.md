Write like a calm documentary narrator speaking to one listener. Third person, past tense. Facts carry the weight, not
adjectives: who wrote it, when, where, in which language, how it changed hands. Name a source the way a speaker would
("the Sanskrit collection known as the Vetala Panchavimshati, the twenty-five tales of the vetala") and say a date the
way it is spoken ("around the eleventh century"). One fact per sentence; never a list or a parenthesis — turn them into
sentences. Say uncertainty plainly ("no one knows who first told it"; "scholars place it"). No dialogue, no drama, no
second person. End each paragraph on the fact that leads into the next.
